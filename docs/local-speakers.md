# Local speaker handling

Whisper Base/Small recognize words. The existing local pyannote/ERes2Net pipeline separately estimates who spoke when. Both run on the Docker server's CPU. Speaker matching and word recognition have different failure modes; a speaker count does not constrain the words Whisper can hear.

Automatic speaker detection favors a small meeting (usually one to three speakers) without imposing a fixed count. It uses a modestly more conservative native clustering cut and consolidates small fragments only when one established voice is a clear acoustic match. Similar simultaneous voices, substantial independent speakers, weak/ambiguous matches, and voices without usable embeddings stay separate. An exact per-recording count of one to ten bypasses this automatic preference. Automatic transcription continues without a count prompt; Retranscribe asks for a count or Auto as before.

A detected voice without a confirmed name appears as Unknown speaker N. The recording-local ID, not the display name, ties its turns together. A clean sample is needed to remember/match a voice profile, but supported anonymous turns retain their numbered identity when no enrollable sample exists. Generic legacy labels get stable display numbers without changing saved words or names. Truly unmatched speech appears as Unassigned speech, rather than claiming that unrelated uncertain passages belong to one person. Use the pencil to name a voice or assign one uncertain excerpt to a person; uncertain audio never trains a voice profile.

Only very short timing holes bounded by the same voice are bridged. Longer gaps, speaker changes and nearby competing voices remain unassigned. Speaker analysis cache version 4 avoids reusing the old grouping policy; completed audio transcription chunks can still be reused after an interrupted job. Existing finished recordings are not automatically retranscribed or reclustered. To apply the new analysis to one, choose Retranscribe and review the existing comparison flow if you have corrections. Changing the speaker-count field alone only sets the next run's preference.

Base and Small, their decoder settings, selected model/language, parallelism, voice-match thresholds, CPU/memory ceilings and model downloads are unchanged in this update. No Medium model or new dependency is added. For word accuracy with these existing choices, select Small when its extra processing time is acceptable and choose the known spoken language rather than Auto when the whole recording uses that language. The effectiveness still depends on the recording's noise, overlap, microphone placement and vocabulary. No numerical accuracy improvement is claimed without a representative labeled recording.

Verification includes the official 16-second two-speaker English sample using the existing CPU models, and deterministic tests of seven fragments of three voices, genuine fourth voices, simultaneous voices, exact-count overrides, missing samples, ambiguity, timing gaps, cache reuse and legacy display. A native cut of 0.8 merged the two-speaker sample; 0.75 preserved both and is used. This small calibration fixture is not a benchmark of the user's meetings.
